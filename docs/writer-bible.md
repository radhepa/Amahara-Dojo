# Dojo — writer bible

This contributor document contains future spoilers. Keep outside the player-facing Site bundle. Revision 1, 4 October 2026.

## Dramatic contract

The player is themselves, a new apprentice. Lantern Hall never makes them an accomplished fighter because they chose the right dialogue. Their power in Chapter 1 is attention, reliability, and asking a question the residents have learned not to ask. The adults already possess skill; neither skill nor kindness makes them infallible. No magical exercise advice. No secretly exceptional bloodline for the apprentice. No five-person orbit of unconditional praise. A missed practice is an absence, not betrayal. Characters have lives on those days.

Martial artists should recognize the negotiation before partner work, restrained contact, permission to decline, mismatched experience, reset after a mistake, coaching feedback, maintenance, and uncertainty under pressure. Action comes from distance, timing, recovery, intent, and partners who adapt. Resonance is a fictional extension, not a substitute for these.

The central question: can a community protect people without taking away their right to decide? Each founder has a smaller version of it. Safety and independence are both legitimate needs. The antagonist offers something useful; refusing him creates real work.

## World and rules

Amahara is a mountain trade town, connected to river settlements and upland passes by old ward routes. Schools are workshops, community halls, sporting clubs, and occasionally civic responders. No borrowed ninja village iconography, franchise techniques, costumes, or ranks.

Resonance is a measurable fictional vibration left by precise coordinated movement in certain old stone and timber structures. It can signal across a route or stabilize a ward for seconds. It does not create energy, heal injuries, read minds, or grant immunity. A misaligned route moves a load elsewhere. Practitioners fatigue and can misjudge. Two trained partners can interrupt a surge more safely than one powerful person. The hall's three-note bell originally announces clearance of a route. Two notes with a missing third are a fault, not a call to destiny. The apprentice notices it; trained adults investigate it. The damaged floor is an access point, not a power-up altar.

Chapter 1 concerns inspection, repair, community use, and a possible old route fault. Town review is scheduled after the season's preparations, not a real-time deadline. Waiting or taking a break never causes an off-screen failure. Story time advances with episodes.

## History — actual canon

Nine years ago the eastern river flooded. The town's centralized ward system protected the market by shifting pressure toward small downstream settlements. The council called it an unavoidable failure. Inspector Natsume signed a narrow report that documented the market's success while withholding the route diagram under emergency secrecy. She thought release would expose vulnerable routes to raiders. She also protected her own judgment.

Iori, Lantern Hall's former caretaker and Daichi's close friend, refused an untrained public demonstration before the flood. Daichi supported him privately but did not confront the council publicly until the responders were dispatched. Iori was seriously injured rescuing people at the spillway. He survived, now lives in the western settlements, and works with his daughter Kanna, a route engineer. He is neither a dead mentor nor secretly evil. He has anger and a life beyond forgiving Daichi. Daichi has failed to send seven drafts of a letter because he imagines a perfect apology can erase his late decision. Iori's red scarf holds a loose beam so the hall can remain in use. It began as a practical fix; everyone has made it a monument without asking him.

Shigure inherited the ward contract after the flood. He believes local discretion cost lives and wants a single accountable training authority. He funds real relief, loves his town, and conceals data showing that the original centralized design caused the downstream harm. His plan will give him control of independent halls' routes and records. He is not responsible for every mishap. No staged injury to manipulate the player. His authority can eventually fracture, reform, or harden depending on public choices.

Sora, formerly a relief courier, delivered an edited copy of the report three years ago. She later noticed missing pagination. She withheld her suspicion to verify it, and because disclosure implicates a person who paid for her brother's schooling. Her brother is alive, not a hostage. Chapter 1 reveals only the missing page and her courier stamp; she admits the connection to the hall in Chapter 2. Her flaw is managing others' knowledge, not an inexplicable secret alignment.

Yuzu came on a six-month travel agreement, is nearing its end, and has family work on the river. She loves the hall and also loves travel. Her resolution is a negotiated return date and a remote contribution, not sacrificing her identity to stay. Ren and Haru love each other but their school and household distribute attention unevenly. Haru fears that enjoying something Ren does well will be patronizing. Akari and Ren have an old attraction held back by her need for control and his habit of turning discomfort into performance. Daichi and Mika have a long companionship, not a forty-year-old romance that only exists when the player pushes it.

## Founders and voices

**Akari, 28.** Goal: keep a safe independent hall that does not depend on one exhausted person. Obligation: inspection paperwork and aunt Natsume. Flaw: treats delegation as a test people will fail. Behavior shift: gives someone a real task without silently doing it twice. Voice: plain exact sentences, dry understatement. Humor: the absurd number of labels she creates; never humiliation of a novice. Stress: shortens words and asks facts before feelings. Hobby: mends clothing beautifully but refuses to admit she likes patterned fabric. Signature: straightens a crooked object while thinking, then eventually leaves it alone.

**Ren, 26.** Goal: be reliable rather than merely exciting. Obligation: neighbor bakery and brother Haru. Flaw: promises quickly and hides disappointment inside a joke. Behavior shift: says he cannot do two jobs, finishes one, asks for help on the other. Voice: concrete enthusiastic images, interruptions, the occasional honest sentence with no joke. Not every line is exclamation. Hobby: excellent baker; careful hands are the same hands he uses in striking. Competence: adjusts range on pads, stops a combination when the holder's balance changes.

**Sora, 25.** Goal: learn facts with other people rather than preserve control by knowing first. Obligation: former courier contacts, sibling schooling. Flaw: can rationalize withholding anything as protection. Behavior shift: shares an incomplete observation and labels uncertainty. Voice: economical, precise questions, quiet observational humor. Not cryptic prophecy. Hobby: annotates maps and cooks recipes exactly once before changing them. Practical affection: leaves a dry towel where it will be needed.

**Daichi, 43.** Goal: make the hall welcoming while having the difficult conversation he owes. Obligation: Iori, safety mentorship, Mika. Flaw: warmth can postpone a boundary. Behavior shift: sends an imperfect letter, admits his part aloud, lets the reply take time. Voice: concrete, gentle, sometimes mischievous; no universal motivational speech. Hobby: tea repair and terrible puns about carpentry. Action: patient clinch frames and weight positioning, pressure released promptly at a partner's signal.

**Yuzu, 24.** Goal: keep both freedom and commitments. Obligation: river family and touring contract. Flaw: makes herself useful without agreeing to anything long enough to be disappointed. Behavior shift: specifies what she can offer and a date to return. Voice: sideways humor, tactile observations, directness when a joke would dodge responsibility. Hobby: folds paper insects, collects local tunes, invents movement games. Teasing must stop when someone dislikes it.

## Supporting cast deployment

Riku (24) appears Episode 1 as a skilled repair collaborator, not a comic incompetent. Mika (42) enters Episode 2 as a community medic with work outside the hall. Haru (27) appears Episode 3, skilled and emotionally awkward. Toma (14) is introduced Episode 2, messenger, strictly nonromantic. Emi (20) visits Episode 4, future recruit with her own decision. Natsume (46) enters Episode 5, inspector, niece Akari's aunt; tough but concrete. Shigure (39) speaks in Episode 3 and returns Episode 7; his proposal is materially helpful. Seiran (31) only in the future roadmap; Kanna is first a letter signatory and becomes an active character in Chapter 4.

No seven introductions at once. At most two new active speakers in an episode; reappearance gives a person texture before adding another.

## Eight-episode causal spine

1. Arrival → discover actual damage → test asking for help → repair decision → communal meal. Player chooses floor-first or welcome-first; safety perimeter maintained in both. Choice changes community work allocation and finale presentation.
2. Routine → neighbors need predictable access → Ren overpromises → first shared responsibility → choose public access or smaller appointments → a room feels lived in. Choice changes Emi's first visit and later queue management.
3. Haru comparison → independent hall proposal → incomplete route diagram → choose to consult Akari openly or Sora privately → bell fault investigated. Consultation changes trust and how Sora later owns disclosure, never creates an objective best route.
4. Open activity → amusing public disagreement → Emi's uncertainty → borrowed bell prop used without checking → preventable embarrassment, not injury → choose public ownership or individual repair → repair trust. Consequence returns in Episode 7's rehearsal.
5. Inspection → Daichi's delayed decision becomes visible → conditions of autonomy → missed commitment → choose a small promise with date or ask shared responsibility → keep it → founder delegation begins. No tutorial tells the player which is more virtuous.
6. Family lives outside the hall → Yuzu's departure intention → scarf comes down during repair → Daichi sends a letter → choose to keep a seat empty or make a place for whoever arrives → group redefines belonging. Emotional payoff is the unused sixth bowl finally being used by a neighbor, not a tragic surprise.
7. Review rehearsal → Shigure's offer would solve costs but transfer control → rain reveals route strain → town commitments pay off → choose founder-led statement or apprentice-and-neighbor statement → practice presentation and delegation. Player does not fight the visitor to win review.
8. Return to opening choices → real small contribution → adults' safe demonstration → independent conditional approval → quiet meal with a reply from Iori → missing page discovered in the repaired floor → choose open investigation or careful verification. Both lead to Chapter 2 with different obligations and distinct confidences.

The hall gets a year of provisional independent use, with clear repair and oversight requirements. It has not defeated all politics. Chapter 1 resolves belonging and immediate survival, not the campaign mystery.

## Relationship boundaries

Five founder personal arcs have four scenes each: introduce a specific habit; expose cost; make a request or disagreement; change a behavior. They unlock with practice count, bond, and chapter context. Repeating clicks cannot farm them. Player choices listen, disagree respectfully, or offer concrete help. None requires sharing trauma. NPC-led disclosure stays meaningful when player selects comfortable/private reflection.

Player romance is optional and adult-only. Late season offers a private, unpressured invitation with a friendship answer of equal depth and reward. One mutual interest at a time in the starter. NPC relationships proceed autonomously through authored scenes. Selecting Akari or Ren suspends their pair's romantic step, not their friendship. If neither is chosen they agree to one walk. Daichi–Mika take a step only if Daichi is not the player's chosen interest. Existing adults can decline gracefully; friendship never penalized.

## Campaign reveal ladder (private)

2: Sora's courier connection; multiple copies of the report. New apprentices expose hall values through actual disagreements. 3: Natsume's signed omission; Haru's school protects responders but teaches unquestioning deference. 4: Kanna establishes physical route causation and Iori's independent account. 5: Shigure's relief depends on undisclosed redirected burdens; early public promises complicate alliances. 6: travel to affected settlements, whose residents are neither victims waiting for saviors nor secretly hostile. 7: new independent network proposed, with real accountability tradeoffs; player now has coached experience but must defer beyond competence. 8: an emergency forces revelation of the original route design; institutions and friends sustain lasting losses. 9: deliberate confrontation over route authority; optional permanent losses only follow legible costs, not random affection punishment. No mandatory founder death planned solely to manufacture tears. 10: build a functioning shared system, accept unresolved relationships and limits; legacy is who can practice safely and belong, not possession of the strongest attack.

## Continuity register

Red scarf: practical support → apparent shrine → Daichi admits hesitation → washed and folded while the letter asks whether to return it → Iori asks for it in his reply. The proper beam brace replaces the tied scarf in later hall art. Sixth bowl: routine empty setting → Mika notices → ep6 neighbor uses → ep8 Iori asks to bring his own next visit. Three-note bell: rehearsal prop → borrowed without check → fault → diagram missing page → trained verification. Ren's burnt tray is one mishap; his baking competence established first and returns during review. Yuzu's paper moth maps a safe open lane and ends up attached to the household calendar. Sora's towel/ink stain are practical clues; not proof of conspiracy. Akari's task labels change from names to shared jobs. Riku's repairs happen before floor access opens. The mystery never asks an apprentice to strike or pry live ward hardware.

Every major choice has an immediate character response, a mid-season remembered consequence, and a finale consequence or explicit pending promise. Comfort, privacy, and rest never imply moral failure. Player skill rank is never changed by story completion.
