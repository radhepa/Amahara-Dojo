# Dojo — story audit and Revision 3 decisions

**AUTHOR ONLY. Major spoilers. Never summarize in owner-facing chat.**

Audited 7 October 2026 against Revision 2 (6 October). The owner asked for the saga to be analysed for faults and holes, then finalized so it feels like a real anime in visual-novel form: breathtaking, emotional, and worth every post-workout episode, fillers included. The owner delegated every narrative decision and must not be spoiled.

Revision 2 was ethically careful and internally consistent. Its problems were genre, protagonist, and payoff. It read like a civic procedural: nearly every beat was a meeting, a custody arrangement, a clause or a ledger, and almost every decision chose between two administrative formats that the register itself said would converge. Revision 3 keeps its world, its ethics and the published Chapter 1, and rebuilds Chapters 2–10 around people, rivalries, set pieces and earned catharsis.

## Faults found and how Revision 3 fixes them

### 1. The player had no arc
The apprentice was barred from every meaningful moment ("protected record station", "may rest", "nobody thanks the apprentice"). The player would do roughly 400 real workouts while their character sat at a desk. The story never acknowledged the training that unlocks it.

**Fix.** The apprentice's fictional training follows the real curriculum chapter by chapter (hands, defense, kicks, combinations, forms, timing, endurance, the Line). They gain a cohort (Emi, Avel, Jalen), a first public showing (Ch5), a first real test (Ch8: holding the hall's anchor for ninety seconds), the climax's most personal decision (Ch9), and a final scene that mirrors their first day. They remain ordinary: one of a thousand, never a chosen one, never certified. The story still never claims the real player gained a rank.

### 2. Solo training and the story were thematically disconnected
**Fix: "Light your own lantern first."** Amahara's halls practise a solo count and solo forms at dawn and dusk before an unlit lantern; partner work is application. Every founder still trains alone daily. The saga's spine becomes *alone, together*: people who practise alone in a thousand rooms are what make a shared line possible. The antagonist holds the opposite thesis: that one will strong enough can carry what crowds drop. The real player, training alone in their own room, is doing exactly what the characters do.

### 3. No present antagonist until Chapter 6, and no personal stakes in the villain
Maela appeared late and her brother's death was unconnected to anyone the player knows.

**Fix.** Maela is seeded in Ch2 (a Veyr newspaper and Avel's memory of her as the hero of Merrow Quay), named on a dispatch in Ch4, and appears in Ch5 with a terrifying solo demonstration. Her brother Tobin died at Merrow Quay on the same flood night that crippled Iori at Weir End. Both harms came from the routing Natsume's signature hid. The saga's tragic web: Natsume signed; Iori and Tobin paid; Maela became the authority so it could never be done to her again, and in Ch8 her refusal to release an anchor kills Natsume. In Ch9 Akari, Natsume's niece, saves Maela's life. In Ch10 Iori, who survived the night her brother didn't, speaks to her at trial.

### 4. The climax had no catharsis
Revision 2's Chapter 9 resolved through reduced loads, observers, petitions and authenticated logs. The duel was refused; nothing was released emotionally.

**Fix: The Unbroken Line.** The Crown Junction lets whoever holds it decide where a surge goes. Cutting it would repeat the original crime downstream. Spreading the load across hundreds of small door anchors, each held on one shared count by people who have practised the base count for a season, makes every share small enough to hold. This is set up mechanically from Ch1 (pairs are safer than singles; timing codes travel; load goes somewhere) and practically in Ch7 (the pilot). The whole cast holds doors across three countries; Ren, unable to stand, rings the count; Daichi and Iori hold the spillway together; refusing conscripts join; Akari saves Maela. The final action combines skills already shown and introduces no new technique.

### 5. The first mystery never paid off
The missing third note was a fault with no emotional destination.

**Fix.** The three notes report clearance from three gates; the third comes from the Old Gate below the market, stuck since the flood and bypassed by a temporary coupling that failed months before the player arrived (matching the published "giving only two for months"). Natsume tells Akari in Ch3 that she once thought she would be forgiven when it rang three again, "now I think it'll only mean the gate works." In Ch8 she frees the Old Gate by hand, the bell rings three for the first time in the player's experience, the last evacuees cross on it, and she dies. Her last words: "It works." Lantern Hall's own bell stays at two through every earlier test so nothing pre-empts the moment.

### 6. Decisions were administrative and convergent
Twenty-seven choices such as guild versus clinic custody or rotating coordinator versus paid office.

**Fix.** Eighteen personal choices with visible, emotional callbacks: whether to tell Avel her hometown is on the map before it is verified (the apprentice faces Sora's own dilemma); where Akari sits at her aunt's confession; who the apprentice spends the Lantern Festival with; whether to read the Merrow names with Natsume; which way to step on the Line's last count, sending the final shudder into the bell or the floor. Both options always remain dignified; large losses never depend on a choice.

### 7. Too many overlapping characters
**Fix.** Lyra Om is merged into Neris Vale (Veyr registrar who brings the ledger, loses her post, then serves as an independent treaty observer). Each remaining newcomer has one clear silhouette and one climactic moment: Avel's speech, Jalen's refusal to surrender a roster, Kanna's call, Elian holding the lane against his former teacher, Tarek's seated count, Neris's authentication.

### 8. Fillers were missing
Revision 2 had almost no pure-pleasure episodes. **Fix.** Every chapter has deliberate fun: label wars, a bake-off, academy sports day, a rookie forms showing, a hot spring, a harbour dunking, the Lantern Festival, kneading dough on the count the night before the Line, a river day in the finale. Each filler still carries a character reveal, a running-gag payoff and one sincere line (see `episode-craft.md`).

### 9. Chapter lengths ignored pacing
Every chapter had 48 beats, including 48 beats of post-war aftermath. **Fix.** Chapters run 6 or 8 weeks (408 workouts in all): shorter chapters for tight arcs, eight weeks for the tournament, the network/festival and the climax; the finale is six weeks.

### 10. The ending lacked a mirror
**Fix.** Keep Revision 2's ordinary final image and make it a bookend: a newcomer at a door that sticks, a bench in the way. The apprentice says the line Akari and Ren once said to them, three notes ring from an inspected route, and when the newcomer asks whether they are a teacher here, they answer: "No. I just practice here."

### 11. Workouts did not follow story intensity
**Fix.** The weekly rhythm pairs the heaviest workouts (Thursday, Saturday) with hinge and climax episodes and the easy days (Tuesday, Sunday) with slice-of-life and quiet episodes. Chapter trial weeks coincide with chapter climaxes.

### 12. Smaller holes closed
- Iori's catchphrase: "Light your own lantern first" was his; Daichi kept saying it for nine years. Iori created the Lantern Form.
- Why Maela knows strategic anchor locations: authorised negotiations (Ch6) and the lawful lease (Ch7), not omniscience.
- Why the Old Gate was never repaired: parts were delayed by the lease's freight priorities (Ch7), a real cause rather than coincidence, and never a player decision.
- Why the evidence cannot be seized: Sora distributes sealed copies to every door before the Line (her arc from hoarding knowledge to distributing it).
- Why ordinary beginners can safely help: each door carries a small fraction of the load by design; trained pairs hold the dangerous sites.
- Toma remains a minor and is never placed in danger.

## What Revision 3 keeps from Revision 2
The world (Hoshin, Veyr, Ordan, the Wayline Accord, the Crown Junction), resonance rules, Natsume's death and its independence from player choices, Mika's hand and Ren's knee, White Ash's destruction with every student saved, Shigure's prosecution, Maela alive and unconverted, Iori's incomplete forgiveness, the Common Routes Compact, no founder death, no secret mastermind, no sequel hook, equal friendship and romance routes, no absence penalties, and the published Chapter 1 as binding canon.
