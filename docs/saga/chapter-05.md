# Chapter 5 — Borrowed Ground

**AUTHOR ONLY. Complete treatment; not yet playable.**

## Chapter contract

Different schools arrive for a parts comparison and a sporting exchange. Their techniques, courtesy, and obligations make the world larger and more enjoyable before the politics harden. The sporting event can establish respect but cannot award control over a river. Shigure's financial disclosures reveal that useful relief depends on a hidden redirected burden. International guests bring evidence, interests, and limits of their own. The chapter ends with a repair coalition under pressure and an invitation to see what Amahara's prosperity looks like from elsewhere.

## Eight episode units

### Episode 1 — The visitor's ground

1. Elian arrives from Tideglass Hall carrying a travel-worn practice frame and a written equipment list. They want a workable exchange, not to assess Amahara's worth.
2. Avel welcomes them in the language she chooses, then refuses an assumption that she must host every foreign visitor. Elian apologizes directly and helps find lodging.
3. Ren admires Elian's changing-ground footwork and overestimates what can fit into a short demonstration. Elian asks which task is actually agreed.
4. Akari states contact limits and isolated-anchor boundaries. Elian requests translation and an equal right to call a stop; both needs alter the printed rules.
5. The event's first resources go to broad accessible hosting or a smaller technical session [C05-A]. Both retain participant safety and a path to the other activity.
6. A good meal establishes pleasure across languages. Avel is allowed to leave early for paid work without the hosts making her absence a cultural message.

### Episode 2 — The ledger from below

1. Lyra arrives with authenticated copies and a chain of custody. She keeps named sources private under an explicit agreement rather than offering total transparency as trust.
2. Her load ledger matches the survey's pattern and shows later shortages. It does not establish that every Veyr crop failure came from Hoshin.
3. Sora wants all supporting names; Lyra refuses. They negotiate independent verification instead of one winning the theme by shaming the other.
4. Kanna cross-checks dates and confirms continuing load transfer after the flood. Shigure's inherited contract cannot now be treated as purely historical.
5. A portion of the ledger is translated into public burden figures. Technical access remains restricted and no source loses protection to create a dramatic reveal.
6. Shigure presents relief receipts that accurately show lives helped. Their truth does not answer how the savings were generated.

### Episode 3 — The price of neutral parts

1. Tarek arrives from Cairn House to inspect a disputed component. He brings access stools before ceremonial gifts, making his school's priorities practical.
2. Ordan's manifest shows isolation gates were available but excluded from the cheap package. Neutral suppliers profited without controlling the final purchase.
3. Riku asks who will fund replacement hardware and ongoing inspections. Tarek admits his hall receives a share of the guild's revenue.
4. Natsume identifies the committee recommendation she accepted. Three countries' separate compliance records are starting to fit one unaccountable arrangement.
5. A joint technical session reproduces the load shift on an isolated model. The foreign practitioners contribute different timing solutions with visible strengths and limitations.
6. Ren feeds the working crews while Seiran gets public applause. He feels envy and still brings the correct number of meals without turning competence into spectacle.

### Episode 4 — The disputed restart

1. Seiran and Elian begin a bounded international exhibition. Differences in foot placement and recovery are visible; no national morality is embodied by the winner.
2. An ambiguous restart translation gives Seiran an early point. Elian calls a stop and the match officials compare the written versions.
3. The point is annulled through the agreed appeal. Seiran accepts the correction angrily but precisely; accepting process need not look serene.
4. The corrected exchange ends in a narrow Elian win. Haru sees that Seiran remains a good instructor when she cannot hide behind a flawless result.
5. Emi halts an overcapacity visitor queue. The earlier hosting choice changes how overflow is accommodated, not whether overcrowding must be prevented.
6. The competitors help dismantle the frame and argue cheerfully over dinner. The relationship carries respect and disagreement into later crises.

### Episode 5 — A helpful clause

1. Neris arrives as a civil treaty officer to discuss replacement parts. She distinguishes Veyr's legitimate downstream claim from her country's emergency Directorate powers.
2. Shigure offers to fund immediate repairs through a centralized purchase agreement. Its conditions include exclusive custody of route records and supply schedules.
3. Mika asks whether clinics can refuse data enrollment and still receive aid. Neris admits the emergency form does not currently protect that refusal.
4. The coalition's leverage is chosen: audit relief spending or build an independent parts fund [C05-B]. Both continue basic aid and verify invoices; progress and allies differ.
5. Daichi declines Shigure's request for a private supportive statement. Their shared civic history makes the refusal painful instead of effortless heroism.
6. Neris offers a civilian review session in Veyr. She cannot promise its result, and the invitation is recorded without making her an instant trusted ally.

### Episode 6 — The money someone else saved

1. The audit or parts fund establishes that deferred isolation spending finances visible relief. Shigure knew after inheriting the contract and repeatedly chose concealment.
2. He publishes selected true receipts before the complete account, casting reformers as people prepared to cancel medical supplies for a principle.
3. Sora's earlier statement appears accurately quoted but stripped of its uncertainty. She issues a specific correction and cannot force every audience to accept it.
4. A household receiving aid disputes the inquiry's priorities. Mika agrees a patient cannot wait for a perfect institution and still opposes compulsory record access.
5. The coalition protects current deliveries through temporary transparent contributions. Riku refuses an unpaid replacement schedule that would transfer the debt to repair workers.
6. Shigure's policy remains lawful pending review, but his credibility divides. Neither a confession nor a sporting loss has removed his material influence.

### Episode 7 — Invitations with limits

1. Tarek asks visitors to see Ordan's supplier vote rather than speak for his country. Elian offers Tideglass lodging with the same independence from official negotiations.
2. Yuzu is expected to translate every itinerary into available time. She names her river contract and asks the coalition to fund another coordinator.
3. The delegation mandate is selected: repair agreements or listening and evidence first [C05-C]. Both forbid committing absent settlements to a route they have not approved.
4. Kanna drafts the downstream veto as a required clause. Natsume helps explain how a future authority could evade it unless suspension is physically possible.
5. Avel elects to remain in Amahara. Her choice is respected; migration does not oblige a homecoming scene for the travelers' benefit.
6. Emi takes a paid welcome shift and Jalen returns to supervised academy preparation. The hall can function while some founders travel.

### Episode 8 — A smaller departure

1. The gathering closes with supplies counted and matches recorded separately from civic agreements. Respect between schools has not erased the financial dispute.
2. Shigure approves ordinary travel permits while warning that dispersed liability may fail. His warning seeds a real Chapter 7 problem rather than empty villain talk.
3. Lyra learns that her official post is under review for bringing the ledger. She chooses to keep traveling while her colleagues protect independent copies.
4. Ren catches up on bakery work and can join only a portion of the trip. No founder is made omnipresent to preserve ensemble convenience.
5. Neris's appointment and Ordan's supplier vote set the route: first the plateau, then the coastal watershed. Delays and dispatch intervals are explicit.
6. Yuzu hands over the hall key and a limited return plan. The people staying behind begin supper before the road party has disappeared from sight.

## Six supporting visits

1. Elian teaches observers how an isolated demonstration's scope is negotiated.
2. Lyra explains custody without exposing her protected source.
3. Tarek describes his hall's ordinary budget and the guild income it relies on.
4. Neris revises one emergency clause under Mika's criticism.
5. Emi sets a capacity and a shift's pay before accepting it.
6. Avel shares a meal and chooses not to travel.

## Handoff

The relief economy's continued harm is documented. A repair coalition exists with a finite fund and competing mandates. The foreign halls are people, not allegories; their governments and suppliers have distinct responsibilities. The next step is firsthand travel, not an invasion sprung without cause.
