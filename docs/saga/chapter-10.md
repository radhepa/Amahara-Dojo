# Chapter 10 — What We Leave Open

**AUTHOR ONLY. Complete final treatment; not yet playable.**

## Chapter contract

The ending takes as much care with the peace as earlier chapters took with conflict. Trials do not repair houses. Reparations do not restore Natsume. Love does not cure an injured hand. Shared authority can become another inaccessible bureaucracy unless people fund and challenge it. Over several explicit story months, the coalition builds the Common Routes Compact, resumes ordinary training, and gives Lantern Hall a life beyond the founders' constant availability. This chapter completes the saga; it does not introduce a replacement enemy.

## Eight episode units

### Episode 1 — A budget is also a promise

1. The reconstruction list names lost buildings, clinical adaptation, ordinary housing, and the dismantled historic asset. Nobody calls every expense a monument to victory.
2. Hoshin's former contract funds enter a supervised restitution account alongside general contributions. Downstream representatives control how their awarded repairs are implemented.
3. First discretionary reconstruction emphasis is chosen: clinic access or household isolation work [C10-A]. Essential care and guaranteed repair funding stay protected in either sequence.
4. Ordan's guild transfers parts without the old hidden profit surcharge. Tarek's hall receives transparent class support rather than losing access facilities to moral symbolism.
5. Veyr civic delegates fund their own repairs and contest emergency powers domestically. Lantern Hall neither installs their government nor claims credit for its entire reform.
6. The preserved archive room or landing opens a practical opportunity; the dismantled asset begins a replacement project. Earlier losses create different work without unequal belonging.

### Episode 2 — The testimony that is not a duel

1. Shigure's hearing distinguishes inherited design from deliberate later concealment and the access lease. His relief receipts mitigate some claims without canceling the harm.
2. Affected residents testify through the Chapter 9 shared address or separate briefs. Private submission remains possible without being treated as weaker grief.
3. Natsume's signed confession and the gate records are presented together. Her death does not erase culpability or permit officials to blame every decision on her absence.
4. Maela's trial considers unlawful occupation, cancelled suspension rights, and the anchor order during evacuation. Her competent supply work is evidence, not immunity.
5. Neris testifies about her own drafting; Lyra explains authentication limits. The process can disagree with part of the coalition's preferred account and remain legitimate.
6. Shigure loses contract authority and receives a lawful restitution and restricted-service sentence; Maela remains in lawful detention after judgment and appeal. No protagonist delivers private punishment.

### Episode 3 — A compact someone can read

1. The Common Routes Compact negotiates public burden limits, funded maintenance, protected technical access, local suspension, bounded emergency terms, independent inspection, and civilian appeals.
2. Kanna rejects a clause that makes downstream veto costly enough to be unusable. Akari supports the correction even when it slows an upstream reopening.
3. Seiran demands qualification standards for operators, while Emi demands plain language for residents. Both requirements are incorporated rather than made rivals.
4. Sora and Lyra separate public decisions from restricted operational schematics. Transparency preserves accountability without turning the player interface into a sabotage manual.
5. Neris obtains Veyr civic ratification through its own delegates; Ordan cantons ratify at different dates. The story allows genuine partial progress.
6. The initial compact enters force with review dates and funded appeal staff. It is an operating institution with failure channels, not a speech declaring universal harmony.

### Episode 4 — People learn somewhere else first

1. White Ash resumes safe classes in a temporary hall before construction is complete. Seiran practices a written refusal appeal with the students who once waited for permission.
2. Jalen begins supervised instructor preparation and records a task he is not yet authorized to teach. The saga cannot grant him mastery through loyalty.
3. Mika uses an adapted tool and delegates a procedure requiring dexterity she no longer has. Frustration and competence occupy the same scene.
4. Ren resumes qualified adapted practice under professional guidance. There is no climactic full-power comeback, and Haru waits to be asked before giving feedback.
5. Toma visits a school and chooses a study interest outside messaging. His household's ordinary future matters as much as the responders' ceremony.
6. The apprentice receives the next available beginner practice within the actual curriculum. Narrative experience never becomes a real-world certification or an advanced exercise assignment.

### Episode 5 — The hall without its five hands

1. Lantern Hall completes its roof and disconnected anchor inspection. Riku certifies only the work he is qualified to certify and receives the agreed payment.
2. The new operational emphasis is chosen: a shared welcome rota or a funded permanent welcome role [C10-B]. Both pay required work, permit refusal, and preserve training access.
3. Emi accepts a defined shift while keeping her shop plans. Avel helps form a paid access and translation cooperative rather than becoming the hall's permanent unpaid bridge.
4. Akari leaves opening responsibility with the new arrangement and takes a walk. A crooked cup survives her absence without signaling danger.
5. Ren keeps a bakery schedule; Sora joins a record circle; Daichi declines an extra task; Yuzu signs a touring contract with actual home periods.
6. A minor booking error is corrected by the new workers without summoning every founder. The room proves its future through a small failure handled well.

### Episode 6 — A visit that is not forgiveness

1. Iori arrives on a day he chose, with his own bowl and a workshop errand. Kanna stays for a different meeting and does not manage his emotions.
2. Daichi asks whether Iori wants to see the repaired beam. Iori agrees, then changes the subject to the cost of the replacement brace.
3. They repair a kettle together and disagree over a measurement. Their present friction is allowed to exist beside the old injury.
4. Iori says he can meet again and still does not offer complete forgiveness. Daichi accepts the actual relationship instead of waiting for a cleansing sentence.
5. Akari visits Natsume's memorial record and leaves an amendment from an affected family. The chosen private or public form remains visible without prescribing her grief.
6. Mika joins supper using the utensil she prefers. Nobody introduces her presence as evidence that the community has recovered properly.

### Episode 7 — A system survives a no

1. The compact's first seasonal test encounters a household stop that would inconvenience freight. Operators isolate the branch, and the paid appeal staff explains the condition.
2. A provisional coordinator asks to overrule the stop using an old emergency habit. Seiran refuses the invalid command under the new charter.
3. Riku's crews fix the access fault with funded parts while Tarek and Elian maintain limited isolated timing. Established action returns at a modest scale.
4. A corrected public account names the delay and who bore its cost. Sora adds a factual amendment before someone else has to compel it.
5. The household agrees to reopen after inspection; the complete bell sequence confirms a route that remained legitimately closed until then.
6. The result is imperfect: freight arrives late and somebody complains. The system has handled disagreement without sacrificing a settlement or installing a new savior.

### Episode 8 — An ordinary open door

1. A final shared afternoon lets local and visiting adults enjoy the hall without a victory demonstration. Elian and Seiran argue about their old match over good bread.
2. Each existing Chapter 1 promise receives a visible object or arrangement: repair board, access sign, record correction, named responsibility, bowl, testimony, and inquiry custody.
3. Founder relationships resolve through chosen friendship or adult romance variants. A date, a walk, or shared tea fits actual schedules; no route grants exclusive ownership of the ending.
4. Yuzu packs a case and leaves a real return plan. Daichi's kettle needs Riku. Akari lets Emi open the ledger. Their growth stays ordinary enough to continue.
5. The apprentice's final place is chosen: help with a bounded welcome task or join the room quietly [C10-C]. A chair, a written note, silence, and rest all belong.
6. A new visitor asks for a chair. Somebody checks available space, the three-note clearance sounds from an inspected route, and the hall welcomes them without asking them to save it.

## Final state

The original crime, continuing financial concealment, international burden, occupation, and central bypass all have answers and consequences. Death, disability, displaced households, and incomplete forgiveness persist alongside pleasure and functioning institutions. Five founders remain distinct people. Lantern Hall's future no longer depends on their constant heroism. The apprentice belongs without being exceptional, romantic, publicly expressive, consistently present, or physically advanced. End here; do not append a secret mastermind, recovered Natsume, darker bell, or mandatory sequel hook.
