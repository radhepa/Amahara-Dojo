# Dojo — episode craft

**AUTHOR ONLY.** How every post-workout episode earns its place. Revision 3, 7 October 2026. Applies to all script writing for Chapters 2–10 and to the Chapter 1 weeks 5–8 expansion.

## The promise to the player

Someone has just finished 30–40 minutes of practice. They are warm, a little tired, and they opened the episode as their reward. The episode must pay that back every time: something changes, someone is seen more clearly, and the last line lands. No episode may be homework, a meeting summary, or a stall.

## Units

- **Chapter:** 6 or 8 story weeks (see `saga-bible.md`).
- **Week (episode unit):** one arc with a title, two required openings, six episodes, and a closing sting.
- **Opening:** 3–5 minute pre-practice cold open before practice 1 (Monday) and practice 4 (Thursday). Awards nothing; never introduces a companion before their real introduction.
- **Episode:** the daily post-practice scene (code `beat`). One completed practice unlocks one episode.

## Weekly rhythm (matched to workout load)

The real plan makes Thursday and Saturday the longest sessions and Tuesday and Sunday the easiest; Wednesday is full rest with no episode.

| Practice | Day | Workout feel | Episode role | Reading target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opening A | Mon | — | Cold open: a hook, a new face, or a question | 3–5 min |
| 1 | Mon | New skill | **Setup**: the week's problem in motion | 8–12 min |
| 2 | Tue | Easy review | **Slice / character**: comedy, a relationship, a running gag | 5–9 min |
| Opening B | Thu | — | Pressure arrives | 3–5 min |
| 3 | Thu | Longest | **Hinge**: the week turns; a reveal or confession | 12–18 min |
| 4 | Fri | Skill + conditioning | **Build**: consequences spread | 8–12 min |
| 5 | Sat | Test-style | **Climax**: set piece, decision, or cliffhanger | 12–20 min |
| 6 | Sun | Easy flow | **Quiet / epilogue**: aftermath, a meal, the sting | 6–10 min |

Hardest workout → biggest payoff. Easy day → pleasure. Players can bank practices; the rhythm is a design target, not a lock. Climax weeks (5.6, 7.6, 8.5, 9.7) may place set pieces on several days; their real workouts are the chapter's strongest sessions within the 45-minute ceiling.

Chapter trial weeks (each chapter's last week) open with a fictional trial scene that mirrors the real self-check, and close the chapter's arc.

## Episode anatomy

1. **Cold open (first three lines).** Start in motion: a body practising, an object out of place, a line of dialogue mid-argument. Where natural, open on the body (someone doing the count, the drill the player just did). Never open with a recap.
2. **One turn.** Something is different by the end: a fact, a relationship, a plan, a feeling admitted.
3. **One image.** A picture the player can hold: a scarf drying, a crossed-out point, a lantern lit, flowers at name thirty-one. If it can be painted, list it for art.
4. **The sting.** The last line lands: a joke paid off, a quiet admission, a door closing badly, a sound. Never end on a list of tasks.

Each week's sixth episode ends with a one-line **"Next time"** teaser in the reader UI (spoiler-safe within the week the player has reached). Each chapter opens with a title card and key visual.

## Episode types

- **★ Hinge** — a reveal, confession or turn. Give it room. One per week.
- **◆ Set piece** — readable action or a public event. Geography first (who is where), then stakes (where the load goes), then timing (the count), then cost (what is lost). Use short paragraphs and white space; let one sound carry a moment.
- **● Main** — plot and character advance together.
- **♪ Slice / filler** — pure pleasure, but never empty. A filler needs (a) a character revealed through play, (b) a running-gag payoff, (c) one sincere line. It must be optional only if explicitly marked; otherwise it is a full episode.
- **☾ Quiet** — aftermath, grief, a meal, an intimacy. Allow silence. Do not make every speaker state the theme.

No more than two consecutive ● episodes without a ♪ or ☾. No chapter without at least four ♪ episodes. Grief chapters (8) replace ♪ with small warmth (a joke that fails, a shared bowl), never with forced comedy.

## Anime grammar to use

- **Rookie awe** (3.1, 5.6): the first time the cohort sees real mastery. Describe the sound and the silence after.
- **Rival chemistry**: rivals finish each other's corrections and hate it.
- **Training payoff**: a skill the player practised chapters earlier decides a moment later (the four-direction step decides the Ch9 sacrifice).
- **Motif return**: the bell, the scarf, the bowl, paper moths, labels, the stuck door, bread.
- **Festival before the storm** (7.6 → 8.1).
- **Everyone on the line** (9.7): cut across many doors in short, rhythmic paragraphs on the count.
- **Bookend** (10.6 mirrors 1.1).
- **Eyecatch stings** at the end of each week (listed in each chapter file).

## Things to avoid

- Meetings as episodes. A meeting may happen inside an episode only if someone's body, feelings or relationships change in the room.
- Consecutive speeches that state the theme.
- Burning food to create vulnerability (once, in Chapter 1, is enough).
- Telling the player how to feel about a choice; virtue scores; "correct" options.
- The apprentice as saviour, chosen one, or certified expert.
- Endings that hinge on attendance; absence penalties; missed practice as betrayal.
- Fake deaths, missing-body teasers, resurrection, last-minute mastermind reveals.
- Instructional exercise claims inside the story. The fiction mirrors the curriculum's vocabulary; the curriculum gives instructions.

## The training mirror

Each chapter's fiction shows the apprentice and cohort practising what the player is currently practising (see the "Training sync" line in each chapter file and the spoiler-free `../training-architecture.md`). Use the same names (stance, guard, the 1-2, the slip, the chamber, Form One, the Lantern Form, the Line). Characters may correct the apprentice's fictional form; they never comment on the real player's form, notes or attendance, and never award rank.
