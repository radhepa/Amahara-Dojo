# Chapter 7 — A Thousand Small Doors

**AUTHOR ONLY. Complete treatment; not yet playable.**

## Chapter contract

The alternative must work before the story can ask people to risk anything for it. A local pilot reveals the weaknesses of shared authority: cost, uneven participation, slow disputes, and tasks everyone thought somebody else owned. Those failures are corrected rather than dismissed as enemy manipulation. Shigure's interim lease creates lawful Directorate access to eastern anchors. The founders see the risk but cannot cancel a public contract by wanting harder. The chapter ends in simultaneous success and danger: the local network works, while the region's strategic access has shifted.

## Eight episode units

### Episode 1 — Permission before connection

1. Kanna holds the Weir End consent meeting. Households can decline a branch without losing ordinary clinic access, testing the promised local veto materially.
2. Riku lists available gates and paid installation hours. The parts pool cannot connect every interested household at once.
3. Pilot order is chosen: access links or maintenance training first [C07-A]. Both retain disconnected safe fallback routes; the decision spends time, not lives.
4. Akari wants a single checklist under her custody. Emi points out that a shared system needs operators who can correct the checklist.
5. Seiran accepts a limited academy operator team with an appeal route. Haru supports her stricter qualification standard while remaining loyal to Ren.
6. The first connection is approved by a household representative, engineer, and operator. Nobody claims the apprentice's story progress supplies any of those qualifications.

### Episode 2 — The job with no name

1. A maintenance check is missed because each partner believed the other held it. The isolated branch remains closed and a delivery waits safely.
2. Shigure identifies the liability gap accurately. Akari cannot answer by saying the central contract committed a worse crime.
3. Jalen proposes a named lead, backup, and appeal with a bounded term. His academy habits strengthen the alternative instead of being treated as moral contamination.
4. The governance emphasis is chosen: rotating coordinators or a paid maintenance office [C07-B]. Either includes published responsibility, qualified operators, and independent review.
5. Sora posts the failure before the success report. The first audience criticizes the pilot and still has enough information to decide whether to remain.
6. The delayed delivery arrives under a corrected assignment. No sabotage twist excuses the coalition's first governance mistake.

### Episode 3 — A bell that may be refused

1. A household calls its local stop during the first live supervised test. The central timing breaks, but physical isolation prevents a downstream surge.
2. Tarek and Elian's operators adapt through established codes rather than impossible spoken messages carried by resonance.
3. Seiran confirms the refusal was valid even though it inconveniences her team. Her academy's provisional rule becomes visible behavior under pressure.
4. Ren keeps the support rota through several uneventful sessions. Nobody cheers, and the reliability is what makes the larger test possible.
5. A full three-note clearance is heard after the household agrees to resume. The signal confirms a condition, never overrides permission.
6. Kanna records both the successful test and its extra cost. The pilot is viable because its burdens can be seen, not because it is cheaper in every respect.

### Episode 4 — An offer to bridge the gap

1. Shigure presents the eastern-anchor supply lease to the river assembly. Directorate engineers receive temporary inspection and operating access in exchange for immediate repair and freight support.
2. Neris asks for mixed custody and a nonrenewable interval. The assembly inserts limits but leaves emergency renewal with the central contract holder.
3. Natsume identifies the bypass the clause preserves, using her own earlier omission as evidence of why technical discretion needs a visible limit.
4. Seiran disagrees with Akari: trained centralized crews may be necessary during winter while local gates remain incomplete. Their alliance holds through a real policy disagreement.
5. The hall's response is chosen: challenge the renewal clause publicly or negotiate independent inspectors into implementation [C07-C]. Neither gives the apprentice power to veto the assembly.
6. Shigure signs the lawful lease believing he can keep its scope bounded. Written inspection access now exists at sites the coalition cannot physically command.

### Episode 5 — A repair paid by someone

1. Directorate crews fix an eastern gate promptly. Residents thank the workers and criticize the founders for offering warnings without the same resources.
2. Avel distinguishes the ordinary Veyr technicians from Directorate policy. She refuses a demand to guarantee what her country will do.
3. Sora finds that emergency renewal could place records in the same custody as operators. Neris agrees the risk is real and cannot unilaterally rewrite the signed lease.
4. The selected challenge yields either a recorded public objection or embedded inspectors with limited access. Both have a practical advantage and a boundary.
5. Mika secures continued clinic privacy through a written local arrangement. The clause will survive only if people can enforce it when pressure arrives.
6. Riku's team finishes a pilot gate with an accessible manual control. The unremediated old municipal gate remains separately on a funded but unfinished list.

### Episode 6 — Lives that do not wait for the plot

1. Yuzu leaves for her agreed river contract with a return route and backup courier. The chapter does not punish her for failing to foresee a war.
2. Ren's bakery receives a real order; Akari asks him to cover extra hall work and he refuses a second commitment he cannot keep.
3. Daichi lets an unresolved coordinator argument wait overnight. The new office or rota handles it in the morning without his private intervention.
4. Emi asks the founders to stop framing every ordinary apprentice task as responsibility for the movement. The hall owes beginners a room, not a cause to serve.
5. Jalen takes a supervised exam component and records a skill still needing work. No civic contribution erases that assessment.
6. The founders share a small celebration without a public speech. This is the life the political system should protect, not filler between meaningful events.

### Episode 7 — Access is a form of power

1. Independent inspectors or public petitioners document Directorate stores at eastern anchors beyond the initial repair scope. The material can support either repair or armed custody.
2. Neris reports that the Supply Directorate has invoked border-security review. Civilian negotiators can still speak but cannot bind military deployments.
3. Elian separates Tideglass operators from the Directorate roster and arranges a civilian return route. Their country contains an opposition before occupation starts.
4. Kanna refuses a proposed central reconnection at Weir End until control limits are tested. The coalition preserves the refusal despite supply inconvenience.
5. Forecasted rain prompts scheduled regional preparation. The event is linked to the authored story calendar, never to missed player practices.
6. Shigure refuses to cancel the lease without replacement supplies. His action is comprehensible, documented, and increasingly dangerous.

### Episode 8 — A successful small system

1. The pilot handles a planned load test and a local stop across several independently operated branches. Its first weakness has a functioning correction.
2. The maintenance-first or access-first choice returns as a different bottleneck, both manageable. The report says exactly which households still await work.
3. Seiran adopts the pilot's refusal procedure for academy field exercises. She retains stricter operator requirements and receives criticism without abandoning the agreement.
4. Lantern Hall's year review begins with shared jobs and paid maintenance, preserving Chapter 1's repair or welcome priority in how the room is presented.
5. A dispatch shows Directorate emergency crews arriving under the lease with armed escorts. The regional government disputes their claimed security scope through formal channels.
6. The final scene holds two records side by side: a working local consent test and a renewal order nobody local can presently stop. The rain begins on the story's next page.

## Optional founder conversations

- Ren: a boring rota; wanting a memorable role; declining an extra job; completing a shift whose only reward is that nothing went wrong.
- Sora: an imperfect public failure report; urge to polish it; asking independent witnesses to approve wording; publishing their criticism beside her summary.
- Daichi: a delayed coordinator reply; wanting to settle an argument alone; agreeing to wait; seeing the actual procedure resolve it differently.
- Yuzu: a packed travel case; guilt at leaving unfinished work; naming a return and substitute; departing without a ceremonial test of loyalty.

## Six supporting visits

1. Jalen assigns a lead and backup for a finite task.
2. Kanna explains a household's refusal without exposing private reasons.
3. Seiran reviews an academy appeal under the new rule.
4. Emi draws a boundary around an ordinary training hour.
5. Natsume marks the old manual gate's funded repair as incomplete.
6. Shigure discusses the lease with a critic and leaves the disagreement recorded.

## Handoff

The distributed design works on a limited scale with honest maintenance costs. Political authority has granted the Directorate strategic physical access. Several mitigation measures exist, but no dialogue choice removes the danger or turns a later loss into the player's fault.
