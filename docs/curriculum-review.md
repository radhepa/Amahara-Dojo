# Curriculum review · Chapters 1–10

**Spoiler-free. Safe for the owner to read.** Chapters 2–10 were written on 7 October 2026 from `training-architecture.md`. Chapter 1's review (5 October) follows further down, unchanged.

## Outcome and limits

Dojo is now a complete **solo** martial arts programme: 10 chapters, 68 weeks, 408 practices, 151 new drills plus Chapter 1's published foundation. No partner, instructor, sparring, contact, equipment or pads held by someone else is ever needed. Every drill has standing, supported (chair, wall or doorframe) and seated versions, and gentle practice earns the same rewards.

It teaches deliberate solo practice of striking, defense, kicking, forms, timing and conditioning, with self-checks and honest trial checklists. It is **not clinically validated**, has not been observed by a qualified instructor, and cannot see the player, so it cannot assess form. Milestones record practice and self-review; they are not ranks or a measure of fighting ability. Solo shadow practice builds technique, coordination, balance and fitness; it does not replace pressure-testing against a resisting person, which this programme deliberately leaves out.

## The style: anime in spirit, real in mechanics

The owner asked for a style that looks and feels like an anime martial artist but works in real life. Its base is Bruce Lee's Jeet Kune Do, together with the boxing and kickboxing it borrowed from. The programme's six principles:

| Principle | What it means | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Still, then sudden | Strikes leave from relaxed stillness with no wind-up | Jeet Kune Do's non-telegraphic lead |
| Home faster than you left | Every strike returns along its own line to guard | Boxing Canada's jab and straight-punch teaching |
| The body throws, the hand delivers | Feet, hips and shoulders turn first; arm or shin rides on the turn | Boxing pivot mechanics; kicking biomechanics review |
| Low line, high spirit | Kicks stay at or below the hip, where they work against a real person | JKD stop-kick to the knee; kickboxing low kicks |
| Zanshin | After every combination: guard home, eyes forward, one breath of stillness | Karate etiquette; boxing's "never drop your hands" |
| Breath is the engine | A sharp breath out on each strike, a long breath out in every rest | Boxing practice; exhale-focused breathing research |

The anime feel comes from presentation that is also good practice: a bow and a sharp-breath "ready" to open every session and a bow to close it; two complete solo forms with a kiai; stillness after every combination; shadow rounds against a vividly imagined opponent; bell-cued reactions where the bell is the opponent stepping in and you answer with a stop-hit or a low stop-kick; and long, steady count blocks.

**Deliberately excluded** (architecture rules): jumping, spinning and high kicks, breakfalls and floor rolls, contact, weights on the ankles, maximal tests. Unsupervised high, spinning and jumping kicks carry real injury risk alone and are also the least useful kicks against a real person.

### Weekly rhythm and feel

Each week follows the architecture's rhythm (Mon new skill, Tue easy review, Wed rest, Thu long, Fri skill + conditioning, Sat test-style, Sun easy flow). A week's feel tag sets the minutes, including 5 minutes of preparation and 5 of cool-down:

| Feel | Mon | Tue | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calm, Taper | 30 | 30 | 35 | 30 | 35 | 30 |
| Build | 35 | 30 | 35 | 35 | 35 | 30 |
| Peak | 35 | 30 | 40 | 35 | 40 | 30 |
| Trial, Recovery + Trial | 30 | 30 | 30 | 30 | 40 | 30 |
| Recovery | 30 | 30 | 30 | 30 | 30 | 30 |

Every practice is 30–40 minutes, leaving at least five minutes under the 45-minute ceiling. Trial weeks are a small deload with the chapter trial on Saturday. Effort guides rise slowly: easy (2–3/10) in Chapters 1–4, easy to moderate with "short sentences" in 5–7, moderate and never gasping in 8–9.

### Session anatomy

Every session opens with a chapter-specific warm-up (shoulders for Hands, trunk and neck for Guard, hips and ankles for Kicks, a quiet small-space version for Practice anywhere, breath and rhythm for Endurance) that ends with the bow and the ready, and closes with a cool-down, a short review and a bow. Saturday self-check blocks ask for one correction rather than more effort.

### Forms

- **Form One** (Chapter 6): 36 counts in two sections, built only from Chapters 1–5: opening bow and ready; the straight line (1-2, step, 1-1-2, parry-jab, side steps); the curve and the kick (slip-cross-hook, rear knee chamber, low push kick, high cover, a final 1-2 with a kiai); closing bow. About 60–90 seconds.
- **The Lantern Form** (Chapter 7): 80 counts in four sections (opening and hands with elbows; quarter-turn pivots to each side with a roll; the low line with the check, low round kick and low side kick; the return with a knee strike and a final kick with a kiai). Turns are front-foot pivots; the feet never cross.

Both are written for a left-foot lead and mirrored for a right-foot lead. Forms run in Form blocks: a slow run, a review, then a run on the bell's steady count.

### Combination menu (Chapter 5 onward)

1-2 · 1-1-2 · 1-2-hook · 2-hook-2 · uppercut-hook · slip-2 · parry-1-2 · cover-1-2 · roll-hook · 1-2-low kick · jab-front kick · check-2 · step back · pivot out. The set sequence is four of these in a fixed order: 1-2-hook and step back; slip-2-hook; parry-1-2 and pivot out; 1-2-low round kick.

## Timing engine

All rests may be extended and any block can be cut short without losing the session. One ring marks every phase change; two rings mark a finished block or the workout limit.

| Code | Name | Length | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Learn | 5 min | 1:00 setup · 3 × (0:30 / 0:30) · 1:00 review |
| B | Build | 5 min | 0:30 setup · 4 × (0:40 / 0:20) · 0:30 review |
| H | Hold | 5 min | 1:00 setup · 3 × (0:20 or 0:30 hold / equal release) · review |
| E | Form | 5 or 10 min | setup · slow run · review · count-tempo run · review |
| C | Round | 10 min | 1:00 setup · 3 × (2:00 / 1:00) |
| F | Cue | 5 min | 0:30 setup · 4 × (0:40 cued / 0:20) · 0:30 review |
| D | Long round | 10 min | 0:30 setup · 2 × (3:00 / 1:00) · 1:30 review |
| G | Count | 10 min | 1:00 setup · 6:00 on the count · 3:00 breathing and review |
| S | Strength / mobility | 5 min | 0:30 setup · 3–4 exercises × 0:40 with 0:20 switches · review |

- **Cue scheduler** (Chapter 7): calls come at random moments 4–8 seconds apart inside each cued round, never in setup or rest and never the same call three times running. Each call is a distinct sound on the existing audio clock (high ping, low knock, double ping) and also flashes on screen with its answer, so the drill works muted. Calls are seeded by the saved session and block, so a reload or resume keeps the same calls in sync with the sound.
- **Steady count** (Chapter 9, and every Form block's count run): soft ticks with a louder count one, scheduled on the audio clock, with a pulsing ring and "count n of 8" on screen. The tempo can be slowed or quickened in steps of 4 per minute and is remembered on the device.

## Safety rules carried into every drill

Complexity before speed, speed before controlled power; strikes stop short of a straight elbow and kicks short of a straight knee; kicks at or below hip height with support always offered and no hopping to recover; slow head movement only; strength work stops 2–3 repetitions before failure; effort stays at "short sentences"; pain means rest or gentle mobility; stop for dizziness, chest discomfort or unusual breathlessness and seek medical advice; drink water and go easier in heat.

## Story gating and milestones

A chapter's curriculum unlocks together with its story: `STORY_READY_CHAPTERS` in `lib/curriculum/index.ts` (currently 1). Until a chapter's story is ready, week progression stops at the last playable week, the technique library and week picker hide later chapters, and later milestones show only their direction. Raise the constant when a chapter's story ships; a test checks that playable training never outruns the playable main story.

Ten solo milestones (Foundations, Hands, Guard, Kicks, Combinations, Anywhere, Form, Endurance, Steady Count, Lifelong) replace the old instructor-led Levels 2–10. Each is earned by completing the chapter's practices and its trial-week check-in. Trial-week check-ins ask for an honest record, not a pass, so a "not yet" never blocks progress or story episodes.

## Sources

Each drill lists its sources in the app. They support general technique and safety points; none of them validates Dojo's exact sequence, doses or timings.

- [AAOS OrthoInfo · Martial arts injury prevention](https://www.orthoinfo.org/staying-healthy/martial-arts-injury-prevention): warm-up, stretching afterwards, learning new moves slowly.

# Chapter 1 curriculum review


Reviewed 5 October 2026. The owner requested review and revision of all eight weeks, clearer directions, an audible timer, and a maximum workout length of 45 minutes. This supersedes the earlier hold on weeks 2–8.

## Outcome and limits

Dojo teaches introductory solo movement foundations: a repeatable stance, relaxed guard, balance, four-direction footwork, distance, and coordination. Its intended benefit is deliberate practice of those specific tasks with form checks and corrections. It is not a complete martial art, a cardiovascular or strength prescription, or evidence of fighting competence. This exact plan has not been clinically validated or observed by a qualified instructor. Strikes, kicks, falling, contact, and higher levels need appropriate instruction and assessment.

The audit found repetitive mobility work, late guard introduction, little lateral progression, and goals for coordination/60-second holds that the assigned blocks did not teach. The revision connects every weekly goal to assigned lessons. Warm-up is now 30 seconds of setup plus 4½ minutes of gradually increasing easy movement, replacing only 90 seconds of movement inside a five-minute interval block.

## Progression

| Week | Objective | Concrete self-check | Minutes Monday–Sunday |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stance, guard, shifts, first steps | Rebuild stance/guard; explain first-foot order | 30, 30, 0, 30, 30, 30, 30 |
| 2 | Spacing and controlled stops | Pause for two counts without an extra balancing step | 30, 30, 0, 30, 30, 30, 30 |
| 3 | Four-direction footwork | Name the first foot and restore spacing | 30, 30, 0, 35, 30, 35, 30 |
| 4 | Guard while moving | Comfortable hands through a small step | 30, 30, 0, 35, 30, 35, 30 |
| 5 | Distance and return | Move the base without leaning | 30, 30, 0, 40, 30, 35, 30 |
| 6 | Backward then lateral sequences | Stop between directions; preserve lead | 30, 30, 0, 40, 30, 35, 30 |
| 7 | Choose and coordinate | Recall foot order; correct one form point | 30, 30, 0, 40, 30, 35, 30 |
| 8 | Review actual skills and support needs | Name one improvement and one instructor question | 30, 30, 0, 30, 30, 30, 30 |

All 48 practices include five minutes of preparation, four to six five-minute technique blocks, and five minutes of cool-down/review. Wednesday is full rest without a check-in. Tuesday and Sunday emphasize easy familiar practice. Complexity increases; speed, power, depth, and hold length do not. Repeat lessons/weeks or rest as needed. Never double up missed practices.

Technique blocks: one minute to read/set up, three rounds of 30 seconds practice / 30 seconds rest, then one minute to review. Repetitions are modest examples, not quotas. Directions define lead/rear foot and step pair, movement order, small step size, facing, pauses, mistakes, and a regression. Each drill includes two form checks and supported/seated preparation. Seated rehearsal teaches posture and order, not standing balance. Gentle practice earns the same rewards; any practice interval can be used for extra rest.

## Maximum duration and timer

New saved sessions have a server-issued deadline 45 minutes after starting, including pauses and block transitions. Preview uses the same ceiling. On expiry, movement ends at partial-practice review. Pausing, resuming, reloads, and retries cannot reset the allowance; elapsed time after the deadline cannot complete another block. The normal plan tops out at 40 minutes to allow transitions. Story reading and optional reflection are outside workout time.

Both timers have a locally synthesized bell: one ring for phase changes, two for block completion or the workout limit. Start, Resume, or Test bell unlocks browser audio. Sound defaults on with a device-local mute preference. Scheduling against the audio clock avoids ordinary background-tab UI throttling. Pause cancels movement cues while the maximum still runs. Visual phase labels remain usable with sound off. Closing the saved-practice dialog leaves timing active; closing the page or sleeping the device cannot guarantee audible delivery.

## Compatibility and checks

New drills use `f2-*` IDs. Existing legacy and `w1-*` plans retain their instructions, durations, and original pause/resume behavior; deadlines are not imposed retroactively. Existing self-checks remain self-reported history, not newly certified skills. No story IDs/paragraphs, schema, authentication, hosting identity, dependency versions, or lockfile changes. Journal and exactly-once rewards retain the existing atomic transaction.

Automated coverage: all 56 week/day combinations, 48 practices, readiness modes, exact time budgets, preparation/cool-down placement, lesson progression, phase/bell boundaries, legacy plan support, and deadline behavior across pauses, reload, late completion, and retries. Standard story/art/dialogue checks and local API account-isolation/reward checks also apply. See project status for final validation and publication results.
